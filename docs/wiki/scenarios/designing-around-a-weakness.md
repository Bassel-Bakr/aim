A scenario is a question you ask your aim. A vague question gets a vague answer, so the design work
happens before the editor opens.

- **Name the weakness first.** "Tracking" is not a weakness; a subcategory is.[^REF-44]
- **Practice needs a defined goal.** That is what separates it from repetition.[^REF-36]
- **One scenario, one demand.** A drill that tests three things diagnoses none.
- **Check for the shortcut.** If the drill can be beaten another way, it will be.

## Start from evidence, not from feel

**A benchmark names the gap.** Your weakest subcategory is the one scoring lowest relative to the
others, which is not always the one that feels worst.[^REF-44]

Your own matches are the other source. Review lost fights and ask whether a decision or your
mechanics lost each one. The mechanical losses tell you what to build.[^REF-10]

**Deliberate practice has a shape.** It is built around a specific, well-defined performance goal,
gives feedback on each attempt, and gets harder as you improve.[^REF-36]

A scenario is how you give that goal a form. If you cannot say in one sentence what the scenario
should be hard at, it is not ready to build.

## Isolate one demand

**Pick the smallest unit that still resembles aiming.** A scenario that mixes target acquisition,
sustained tracking and a movement demand produces one number covering all three.

When that number moves, you cannot say which part moved. Splitting the drill in two costs a few
minutes and makes both halves readable.

**Keep the rest of the scenario boring.** Anything not under test should be as easy as it can be
without becoming unrealistic. A weapon with spread, a reacting bot or a cramped map all add noise.

Balance still matters across a week. Isolating one subcategory for a session is not a reason to let
the others rot, which is why a playlist draws from more than one.[^REF-8]

## Find the shortcut before your reps do

**Ask how you would cheat the drill.** A tracking scenario beatable by standing still trains
waiting. A switching scenario where targets queue up in a line trains a memorized sweep.

The test is whether the intended skill is the bottleneck. If some other skill, or patience, sets
your score, the scenario is measuring that instead.

**Predictable motion is the usual culprit.** A path you can learn stops being read and starts being
recited, which is a different skill from reacting to a target.[^REF-15]

That is not always wrong. A repeatable path is exactly what some tracking work wants. It only
becomes a problem when you meant to train reading and trained recall instead.

## Common mistakes

- Building a scenario around what feels hard rather than what scores low.[^REF-44]
- Copying a hard scenario and making it harder, without asking what it isolates.
- Testing three changes in one version, so the result explains nothing.
- Training the isolated weakness so exclusively that another one opens up.[^REF-8]

## In practice

**Write the sentence before you open the editor.** One line naming what the scenario should be hard
at. If you cannot write it, the design is not finished.

**Do this next.** Run a benchmark, take your lowest subcategory, and write one sentence describing
the drill that would isolate it.
